1. Getting started

New to BoatRun? Start with the Quick Start. It walks you through installing the app, first-time setup, and recording your first session, for whichever role you are - Row, Cox, Erg or Coach. About five minutes, no settings to change.

This guide is the full reference. The two tables below are the parts worth reading on your first day out.

2. Mounting the phone

Orientation

Portrait or landscape both work. What matters is that the phone is flat, screen up, and rigidly attached to the hull. The newer phones (iPhone 15 and newer, and most recent flagship Android handsets) have dual-band GPS which is significantly better at tracking. Due to the reflection of signal from water, carbon fibre and metal components, placing a rubber foam mat under the mount increases performance, particularly on older phones.

  • Portrait - the app uses the Y axis for surge
  • Landscape - the app uses the X axis for surge

Calibration works out which one you're using and pins it. You don't have to tell it anything. If the calibration fixes to the wrong axis, you can force a flip to another axis in system settings instead of keeping AUTO mode.

Tilt

Flat is best. The app measures your mount angle during calibration and compensates for it, but the compensation gets less reliable the further you go.

  • 0–10° - no meaningful loss of accuracy
  • 10–30° - works, accuracy degrades gradually
  • Over 30° - not supported; the surge signal gets mixed with heave and metrics become unreliable

If the app detects that your current tilt is more than 8° different from your saved calibration, it will offer to recalibrate before you start. Take the offer - it takes 8 seconds.

Cox mode

In Cox role the phone is facing the bow instead of the stern. If you fail to select cox mode, the primary indicator will be unusual stroke curves as shown here.

Position in the boat

Closest to the rigger gives the strongest stroke signal. Anywhere rigid is workable. What ruins it is a mount that flexes or rocks - any movement between phone and hull corrupts every accelerometer metric, and a mount that wobbles near stroke frequency will corrupt stroke ratio and Stroke Char specifically.

Recalibrate whenever you move the phone, change its angle, or switch boats.

3. Roles - Row, Cox, Coach, Erg

One app, four modes. Set at launch, changeable in HOME → SETTINGS → ROLE.

The SQUAD tab only appears in Erg role, and only once you have actually joined a squad.

4. Erg mode

BoatRun talks to a Concept2 PM5 over Bluetooth. The phone is a second screen and a recorder - it is not measuring anything itself. No GPS, no accelerometer, no calibration. Every number you see comes off the monitor.

Governing principle: wherever the PM5 supplies a value, BoatRun displays it as-is. It does not recalculate pace, power or anything else. If your erg screen and your phone screen disagree, that is a bug worth reporting.

Connecting

HOME → SETTINGS → ERGOMETER (CONCEPT2 PM5) → CONNECT TO ERG, or the CONNECT ERG button on the erg live screen.

  1. Wake the PM5 first - press any key or take a stroke. A sleeping monitor does not advertise and will not be found.
  2. Tap CONNECT TO ERG and pick your PM5 from the list.
  3. Once connected the card shows live rate, watts and drag factor.

CONNECT ERG and CONNECT SQUAD both stay on the erg screen for the whole session. Connected ones turn green and become disconnect controls, so there is always a way back without going into Settings.

Heart rate - pair the strap to the erg, not the phone

Two Bluetooth links from one phone fight each other, and the strap always loses. So on the erg go to Menu → More Options → Utilities → Heart Rate and select your strap there. BoatRun then reads heart rate through the erg connection it already has, and everything downstream - session HR, TCX export, squad sync - works exactly as it would with a phone-paired strap.

The HR button is removed from the erg live screen for this reason. Once the PM5 has seen your belt once, BoatRun remembers the belt ID and asks the erg to re-pair itself on later connects.

PM5 heart rate updates about once a second, slightly coarser than a direct strap. Fine for erg work.

What is on screen

Distance is always in whole metres in erg mode regardless of your km/mi preference, because that is what the monitor shows. DF 124 beside the header distance is the drag factor - a setup reference, not a live metric.

Erg tiles are instantaneous values: pace, rate, watts, drive length, peak force, calories, DPS, drive time, work per stroke, cal/hr, speed, recovery time, HR. Cumulative and averaged figures - split pace, average watts, average force, projected time, projected distance, last split, drag factor - live behind the selectable tile rather than taking permanent slots.

The CURVE tab on the erg

The erg curve is the PM5's own drive force curve in newtons - the drive phase only. It is a completely different quantity from the on-water acceleration curve, on different axes, so the two never mix. An erg stroke template will only draw over an erg curve, and an on-water template only over an on-water curve.

Force curve data is only requested while the CURVE tab is actually open. Polling it every stroke starves the Bluetooth connection and makes every other figure lag, so it is deliberately gated.

Workouts

Load a BoatRun workout the usual way and it is pushed onto the monitor. From then on the PM5 owns the timing - there is no 5-4-3-2-1 countdown screen, no lap summary card, no splash. The erg prompts you, and BoatRun follows along in the INTERVALS panel.

Workouts programmed on the erg itself are mirrored the other way: BoatRun reads the current interval's target off the monitor the moment the workout is armed, and tracks it. It cannot read the total number of reps for an erg-programmed piece, only the interval you are on, so it infers the pattern from the splits you have completed.

Limitation: a new workout programmed on the erg is only picked up when the monitor has returned to its ready state. Program a new piece without going back to ready and the old interval table may hang around.

Not every shape can be pushed. If a workout will not go across you get an explicit message naming the reason rather than silence.

The SQUAD tab - racing

Join a squad on the erg page or HOME → SETTINGS → SQUAD CODE exactly as an on-water rower would. A SQUAD tab then appears alongside CURVE.

Who needs what: joining a squad erg race or training event needs Pro. Setting one up - creating the race, pushing the workout, running the count-in - needs the Coach add-on. See Basic, Pro and Coach.

How a squad piece runs:

  1. Warm up however you like. When you are done, press menu to finish your warmup session. You MUST be on MENU to receive the workout
  2. Your phone shows WORKOUT RECEIVED. Your workout is now ready to go.
  3. The coach starts everyone together with a count-in of about 15 seconds.

During the race the SQUAD tab shows a simulated track with every rower's boat, plus name, pace, rate and the gap in metres ahead (+) or behind (−) you. Everyone resets to 0m during rest.

  • Relative position needs a coach online and a comparable piece. Time intervals compare metre for metre; distance and calorie pieces cannot, so those fall back to pace and rate only.
  • Tap your own name to hide yourself. It is a two-way trade - the squad loses sight of you, and you lose sight of their positions and gaps. Your coach still sees everything. Tap again to rejoin.

Erg data afterwards

Erg sessions save, sync and export like any other session. The Analyser converts the PM5 force curves onto its normal curve grid at load time, so curve comparison, group averages and the video overlay all work - the axis simply reads force in newtons instead of acceleration. Speed integration is skipped, because integrating newtons would produce nothing meaningful.

If something looks wrong

Beta builds have SETTINGS → ERGOMETER → RAW PACKETS, which shows every incoming packet with its byte length, hex and decoded values. If a tile reads oddly, open that, compare against what the erg’s own screen says, and send the dump with the feedback - hex plus two known values is enough to pin the problem exactly.

5. Signing in - your account and your storage

There are two of these and they do genuinely different jobs. Once you see the difference it stops being confusing:

  • Your BoatRun account - who you are. Carries your subscription.
  • Your Google Drive - where your files go. Storage, not a login.

You can have one, both, or neither.

Your BoatRun account

HOME → SETTINGS → ACCOUNT → ACCOUNT & PLAN…

Two buttons, either will do:

  • Continue with Apple
  • Continue with Google

Pick whichever you prefer. What the account is for:

  • It carries your subscription between the app and the Analyser
  • It's how the Analyser knows you've paid to unlock the features

Without linking your account, a subscription bought on your phone stays on that phone and the Analyser won't recognise it.

Adding the second provider. Once you're signed in, the panel offers to link the other one - Apple if you signed in with Google, Google if you signed in with Apple. Optional, and it takes one tap. Worth doing: it's a spare key to the same account if you ever lose access to the first.

Do it deliberately, because your Apple ID and your Google account almost certainly use different email addresses, so they are not recognised as the same person automatically.

Your Google Drive

The button in the top bar. This is not a second account - it's you granting BoatRun permission to write into a private folder inside your own Google Drive.

  • Every finished session uploads there automatically
  • The folder is hidden - it does not appear in your normal Drive file list, and no one can read it or access it except you
  • The Analyser is the only other thing that can manage that folder, once you sign in to the same Google Drive account
  • Skip Google Drive sync and sessions live only on the phone, or are exported from the app via TCX, FIT or JSON

Recommendations

  1. Connect Drive first. This is the one that protects your data - without it there's no backup and the Analyser has nothing to open.
  2. Then sign in to your BoatRun account, with whichever of Apple or Google you prefer.
  3. Then link the other provider. One tap, spare key.
  4. If you only ever do one thing, do Drive.

Live squad data

Only sent while you have a squad code set and connected. Disconnect in Settings and nothing leaves the phone. Position and metrics are sent - treat a squad code like a shared password.

Clearing things out

Everything lives under HOME → SETTINGS → YOUR DATA.

  • STORAGE… - the safe one. Reports what BoatRun is using and offers to free space without deleting anything. Full detail below.
  • DELETE MY DRIVE DATA - permanently deletes every BoatRun session in your Drive. Sessions on the phone are untouched.
  • PRUNE SQUAD SESSIONS (Coach mode only) - removes squad cloud records older than 21 days for the squad code you are connected to.
  • Deleting one session - swipe it left in the LOG, or use the trash icon in the Analyser. This deletes it everywhere. See below.

Deleting and freeing space are now two different things. STORAGE… frees space and loses nothing. DELETE removes the session for good, on every device. If all you want is room on the phone, never use DELETE.

Sync with Drive

The ☁ Sync with Drive button at the top of the session log brings down every session in your Drive that this phone doesn't already have. That's how you set up a new phone, or recover after reinstalling the app.

The first sync on a new device has to fetch every session in full and can take several minutes on a large archive - it will tell you how many it's about to download and let you back out. Do it on wifi and leave the app open while it runs. Afterwards it's near-instant, because the app keeps a small index in your Drive and only fetches what's actually new.

Your Drive copy is the one that always survives. Phones reclaim storage when they get full, and iOS can clear an app's cached data - if that happens, the session detail is simply fetched back from Drive when you open it. As long as you're signed in and syncing, nothing is lost.

How sessions are stored on the phone

A full session - every stroke, every acceleration curve, the GPS track - is around three quarters of a megabyte. A season of rowing is tens of megabytes, far more than the small, fast store the session list is drawn from can hold.

So BoatRun splits every session in two the moment it is saved:

  • A summary - date, distance, time, average pace, stroke count. A couple of hundred bytes. This is what the LOG list draws, and every session you have ever recorded keeps one, forever.
  • The full session - all the stroke and GPS detail, held in a separate database on the phone and loaded the moment you open that session.

You should never notice this. The log opens instantly however many sessions you have, and tapping one loads the detail straight off the phone - no network needed, so it works mid-river with no signal.

Freeing up space - SETTINGS → YOUR DATA → STORAGE…

This is the one button to know about. It first reports what BoatRun is actually using:

  • Total in use, and what share of the space the phone is willing to give the app
  • How many sessions are in the log, and how many of those still have their full copy on the phone
  • How many of those full copies are backed up on Drive, and how many are the only copy on this phone
  • Raw sensor traces and export data held separately, and leftovers from older versions of the app

Then it offers to free space. It removes the on-phone copy of sessions that are older than 30 days and confirmed present in your Drive, and tells you roughly how much that will save before you agree to it.

Nothing is deleted. Every one of those sessions stays in your log exactly as before. Open one and it downloads again from Drive - which does mean you need signal and a linked Drive to reopen an older row. Anything from the last 30 days is left whole on the phone, because that is the stretch you are most likely to want at the shed with no reception.

A session that is not on Drive is never touched, however old it is - for those the phone holds the only copy. If the report warns you that some sessions exist only on this phone, sync before freeing space.

If there is nothing old enough to free, it simply says so.

If the phone fills up anyway

BoatRun will not let full storage cost you a session. If it can't fit one when you press FINISH, it works through these steps on its own, in order:

  1. Strips the raw GPS/IMU trace data from stored sessions - every displayed metric and every export is unaffected; this only drops the debug-level trace.
  2. Removes the on-phone copy of already-synced sessions, oldest first, stopping as soon as there is room. Those sessions stay in the log and come back from Drive when opened.
  3. If there is still no room, the session is kept safe in an autosave slot rather than being lost, and you are told to free space and press Finish again. Do not close the app at that point.

Unsynced sessions are never removed automatically. Connect Drive and keep it syncing, so this process always has something safe to clear.

Deleting a session

Deleting anywhere deletes everywhere. Swipe a card left in HOME → LOG and tap the red DELETE, or use the trash icon in the Analyser, and it goes from that device, from your Google Drive, and from every other device on its next sync.

Use this only when you genuinely want the session gone. To make room on the phone, use STORAGE… above instead - it frees the same space and keeps every session.

There is no undo. The file is removed from the hidden app folder in your Drive, which is not the same as Drive's normal bin - it cannot be recovered from there afterwards. Both apps therefore ask you to confirm first, and the phone names the session in the confirmation so a mis-swipe is caught before it does any damage.

If you delete while offline, the session goes from the phone immediately and the Drive copy is removed the next time you sync. The app tells you which of the two happened.

The DELETE MY DRIVE DATA button in SETTINGS – YOUR DATA wipes every session in your Drive at once, and is the only bulk delete.

Export anything you want to keep before deleting it. Raw data export is free forever - TCX, FIT, CSV and JSON - so a session you export is yours regardless of what happens to your Drive or your subscription.

7. Basic, Pro and Coach

Free trial

Every new install gets 30 days of full Pro, granted by the app. No card, no sign-up, nothing to cancel. The Settings → SUBSCRIPTION card counts down the days.

When it expires, you drop to Basic. Nothing is deleted and nothing is locked away - all your recorded sessions, all your exports, all your raw data stay fully accessible as long as you keep your Google Drive or your exported JSON files.

What Basic gives you

  • Pace, Stroke Rate, DPS and Heart Rate tiles
  • All the selectable-tile fields (time of day, elapsed, lap time, distances, average and lap pace)
  • 20 minute recording limit per session - you get a warning with 2 minutes left, then the session finishes and saves itself
  • Full session log, full Drive sync, full restore
  • Every export - JSON, CSV, TCX and FIT - always free
  • Analyser: single-session analysis, always free
  • Viewer: always free, always full

Raw data is never held hostage. If you recorded it, you can export it.

What Pro adds

Pro lifts the recording cap and unlocks every stroke metric, the live stroke acceleration curve on the water and on the erg, ideal stroke template overlays on the water and on the erg, custom workouts on the water and on the erg, squad erg mode with the live race track, and automatic Strava sync.

On the same account it also unlocks the Analyser properly: multi-session comparison, the video overlay, opening a whole squad at once, and the full stroke template workflow - build one from your own strokes, edit it, export it to the phone, and draw it on the video overlay.

Pro lets you take part in a squad erg race or squad training event. Setting one up is a Coach job - see below.

What Coach adds

Coach is the add-on for anyone running a crew or a squad. Coach includes Pro, so you don't buy both. It adds:

  • Create squad codes - generate a code, name the squad, share it by QR for rowers to scan
  • Password-protected squad codes - keep your squad's live data to your squad
  • Live squad map - every connected rower as a labelled dot, your own boat shown separately
  • Rower tiles - name, big stroke rate, big pace, plus whichever secondary metrics you choose, in a grid you can scale and reorder
  • FREE ROW / RACE switch - flip the lower half between individual tiles and a ranked squad race panel
  • Rower detail on tap - full metrics, live stroke curve, mini route map and a lap button for that one rower
  • REQUEST CURVE - pull any rower's live stroke curve on demand, 5-60 seconds, without draining their battery all session
  • LAP - ALL BOATS - start an interval on every boat at the same instant
  • Create and push workouts to the whole squad, on the water or on the erg
  • Set up squad erg races - build the piece, arm every monitor, watch the n/n LOADED readiness count, then start everyone together with a synchronised count-in
  • Live race leaderboard - every rower ranked with pace, rate and metres ahead or behind, on a simulated track
  • Push a stroke template to the whole squad or one rower mid-session, and hide it again remotely
  • In-app video - record a clip tagged to that rower and that moment, saved to a cloud folder and dropped straight onto their session in the Analyser
  • Collate and export every rower's session from one place
  • Coach from an erg - push and start a squad piece without leaving the coach screens; your own machine arms and starts with everyone else

Rowers do not need Coach. One Coach subscription on your phone covers the whole squad - they join free with the code.

8. Heart rate monitor

BoatRun connects directly to a Bluetooth heart rate strap - Polar H10, Garmin HRM, Wahoo, anything speaking standard BLE heart rate. Connect on the live screen prior to start, or in the settings menu.

Connect before you press Start. The app reconnects automatically on later sessions once it knows the strap, but the first pairing should happen on dry land.

HR appears as its own tile with a zone bar, is recorded into the session, exports in TCX and FIT, and charts in both the session detail and the Analyser.

Zones are configurable - the app supports Friel/Coggan LTHR zones, percentage of max HR, and age-predicted. The zones are synced via your Google Drive and are available in the header of the Analyser website during workout review.

9. Recording a session

The LIVE tab

Your main display of user selectable metrics. The layouts menu defines the variables displayed here.

The CURVE tab (Pro)

The acceleration curve for one full stroke, catch to catch. Last stroke in blue, 5-stroke rolling average in orange. Green fill above zero acceleration, red below zero acceleration.

Three figures sit on the curve itself, each a 5-stroke rolling median:

  • Drive Accel, top left - the highest point of the curve, peak acceleration during the drive
  • Recovery Accel, top right - peak acceleration during the recovery
  • Catch Accel, a third of the way in from the left, bottom of the curve - the lowest point of the curve, peak deceleration at the catch. This one reads as a negative number

Four tiles underneath. Tap on each one to bring up a selection of available metrics.

Stroke templates on the live curve (Pro)

Who can do what with templates: Pro covers the lot - building and editing a template in the Analyser, exporting it, importing it onto the phone, arming it on your live curve, and drawing it on the video overlay. The only template job that needs Coach is pushing one out to a squad mid-session.

A template is a target stroke shape built in the Analyser - see section 15 for how to make one. On the phone it is drawn as a dashed line behind your live curve, continuously re-timed to whatever rate you are actually rowing, so you are always comparing like with like.

Getting templates onto the phone

HOME → SETTINGS → STROKE TEMPLATES → IMPORT TEMPLATE…. Pick the JSON file the Analyser exported. Re-importing a file you already have replaces it rather than making a duplicate.

The list underneath is split into ON-WATER and ERG, each headed with how many are armed - 2/3 ARMED, for instance. Each row shows a colour chip, the name, and how many rates it holds, their range, and its size. Rename with ✎, delete with ×. The running total sits at the bottom and turns orange once the library gets large. Templates live on the phone only - they are not synced.

Arming and showing them

You get three on-water slots and three erg slots, kept separately - arming three on-water templates never crowds out your erg ones. Arm a template either by tapping its row in that Settings list, or by tapping the TEMPLATES button at the top of the curve and picking from there. Both lists are the same set of slots.

Each armed template gets a coloured pill in the corner of the curve, and its dashed line is drawn in the same colour as its pill. Tap a pill to draw that template, tap it again to take it off. This works without leaving the live screen, so you can switch targets mid-piece.

Only the matching kind is ever shown: on-water templates on the Row and Cox curve, erg force templates on the Erg curve. The other set stays armed, waiting for the mode it belongs to.

Reading it

Between the rates in the template the shape is interpolated. Above the highest rate it holds, the drive keeps its recorded length and only the recovery is compressed to fit - so a template built at 18-30 still gives you something sensible at 34.

Templates from your coach

If you are connected to a squad, your coach can push a template to your phone. It arrives as a fourth pill labelled COACH in purple. Tap it off if you do not want it on screen - your coach sees that you have hidden it. The coach can also hide it remotely.

Templates are boat-class specific. A single scull template means nothing in a quad or an eight.

The INTERVALS tab

Shows the step sequence and per-interval metrics as you complete them.

Laps

Tap LAP to mark a split. Each lap records time, distance, pace, rate and HR.

The lap button changes colour

Blue - LAPNormal split, or advance to the next workout step. Takes effect the instant you press it.
Light blue - LAP READYPressing will split and arm ready mode instead of just splitting. The button offers this whenever the boat is sitting still (no workout loaded), or when the next workout step is a row-when-ready step.
Light green - READYReady mode is armed. The lap clock is held and waiting for you to start rowing.

Ready mode

Press the light blue LAP READY button while sitting at the start. The previous lap closes there and then, and the new lap does not begin. Sit as long as you like, back the boat down, line up on the buoy - none of that stationary time lands in your work piece.

The new lap starts on its own after two strokes at a real rate. It backdates itself by about 2 seconds so the very first stroke of the piece belongs to the piece, not to the sitting-around before it. A single bump - the boat rocking, catching the pontoon, shifting on the seat - will not trigger it; the first 2 seconds after arming are ignored entirely, and if the rate is very low it waits for four strokes rather than two.

If you would rather not wait for the detection, press the green READY button and the lap starts immediately. It then goes straight back to light blue, because the boat is still stationary and ready mode is on offer again.

With a workout loaded

Timed and distance steps advance on their own - work for 1000m, rest for 4 minutes. Open-ended steps do not, so press LAP to end a warmup or an open rest period.

How the first work step begins is one choice made on the workout itself, not on the step - see section 11. Set to AFTER 5 SEC, pressing LAP at the end of the warmup runs a 5-4-3-2-1 countdown into the piece. Set to WHEN ROWING, the button shows LAP READY and the same two-stroke gate applies: the step waits, the workout bar reads READY / ROW!, and the countdown only begins once you are actually rowing. Press the green READY button to force it to start.

Everything after that first step runs straight through without waiting.

Stopping and finishing

STOP pauses, RESUME continues, FINISH ends and saves.

The app autosaves at least every 2 minutes. If it closes mid-session (battery, crash, anything) the recording is recovered next time you open the app.

Battery - read this

Connecting to a squad costs a lot of battery. It keeps a live network connection open for the whole session, on top of GPS, the accelerometer, and a screen that never sleeps.

How often your phone transmits depends on what's happening:

SituationSends every
Not rowing60 seconds
Rowing, coach not detected30 seconds
Rowing, coach online9 seconds
Coach has requested your stroke curve3 seconds

The app deliberately backs off when nobody is listening, and it batches samples between sends rather than waking the radio constantly. But a two-hour squad session with a coach connected will use noticeably more battery than the same row on your own - expect up to double the power consumption.

Practical advice:

  • Start every squad session at 100%
  • Turn the screen brightness down - it's usually the single biggest drain
  • Carry a battery pack for long mornings or multiple sessions
  • If you don't need live monitoring, don't set a squad code. You can still Send to coach afterwards, which costs nothing during the row.
  • The coach's own phone drains fastest of all - it's receiving from everyone

Stroke Templates

A stroke template is a set of averaged stroke curves - one per stroke rate - saved as a small JSON file. It becomes a target shape you can draw behind any other curve, in the Analyser or live on the water.

Building one (Pro)

Creating, editing, loading and using templates are all Pro, including drawing one on the video overlay. The only template job that needs Coach is pushing one out to a squad mid-session.

  1. Drag a selection on the chart over a block of strokes at one steady rate → ＋ Add to curves. Repeat for each rate you want in the template - e.g. 18, 22, 26, 30, 34.
  2. Click Create / edit template, give it a name.
  3. Add each pinned curve group in turn. Each one becomes a rate entry showing its rate, its drive percentage and how many strokes it averaged.
  4. Drag the preview slider to see the template re-timed across the whole rate range before you save.
  5. Export writes the JSON file.

Between the rates you supplied the shape is interpolated. Above your highest rate or below your lowest, the drive portion keeps its recorded length and only the recovery is stretched or shortened to fill the stroke - which is what actually happens on the water.

Two kinds of template

Templates are either on-water (normalised catch-to-catch acceleration curves, from the phone's accelerometer) or erg force (PM5 drive-force curves). They are different shapes on different axes and never mix. A template built from on-water data will only ever draw over on-water curves, and an erg one only over erg curves.

18. Metrics reference

Metrics are colour-coded by what influences them:

  • 🟢 Independent - compare across any session, any conditions, any boat. Derived from accelerometer timing.
  • 🔵 Boat dependent - a heavier boat gives lower readings for the same force. Compare within the same boat.
  • 🔴 Condition dependent - GPS-derived. Tide, current and wind all move the number. Compare only in matched conditions and direction.

🔴 Pace

Boat speed, in your chosen unit. GPS Doppler speed over a sliding window. Suppressed when GPS accuracy is worse than 25m or the boat is barely moving.

Use: interval targets, lap-to-lap comparison within a session. Do not compare across days on tidal water unless conditions and direction match.

🔴 Moving Pace

Pace averaged only over the periods the boat was actually moving. A more honest average for sessions with stationary rests.

Use: the better number for interval training. A big gap between moving and average pace means a lot of time sat still.

🔴 Distance Per Stroke (DPS)

Boat travel per complete stroke cycle. Typically 7–11m in a single scull depending on rate and conditions.

Use: at equal pace, higher DPS means you're achieving it at a lower rating. A mid-session drop at steady rate usually means fatigue.

🟢 Stroke Rate

Strokes per minute, from the median of recent catch-to-catch intervals, backed up by autocorrelation when the catch detector goes quiet.

Use: the control variable for structured work. Clears after a rest and returns after two new catches.

🟢 Stroke Ratio

Recovery time ÷ drive time. Higher means a longer, more relaxed recovery relative to the drive.

  • 2.0–2.5 at training rates
  • 1.5–1.8 at race rates

Use: the clearest sign of a hurried stroke. If ratio collapses as rate climbs, you're shortening the recovery instead of quickening the drive.

🟢 Stroke Char (%)

Where the drive force is centred in time. 0% = all at the catch, 100% = all at the finish. It is the impulse centroid - every sample of the drive acceleration curve is weighted by how much force it carried, rather than reading off the single biggest sample. That makes it far steadier stroke to stroke, because a good drive has a broad flat top where the exact peak sample wanders.

  • Under 45% - front-loaded
  • 45–55% - mid-drive
  • Over 55% - back-loaded (or soft/late catch)

Use: rising Char through a session means the catch is going under fatigue. Pair it with Positive Catch Slope as high slope with low Char is clean front-end loading.

Note: earlier versions used the single peak acceleration sample instead of the centroid. Values from older sessions read lower and are noisier - don't compare the two directly.

🟢 Recovery Loss

Speed lost to deceleration during the recovery, normalised per second of recovery so the number is comparable across stroke rates. Accelerometer-derived, condition-independent. Lower is better.

Use: high Impulse with high Recovery Loss means energy is going into deceleration - often the rating is too low for the force you're putting in. Low Impulse with low Recovery Loss is an efficient, consistent drive.

🔵 The catch group

Four metrics describe the same feature of the stroke curve: the check, the trough the hull drops into as the blades go in. Read together they tell you how you entered it, how deep it got, how you came out of it and how long the whole thing lasted.

  1. Negative Catch Slope the gradient going down into the check
  2. Catch Accel the depth of the trough
  3. Positive Catch Slope the gradient coming back out of it
  4. Catch Dur the width of the trough

Both slopes are reported as positive numbers because they are magnitudes. A larger Negative Catch Slope means a steeper fall, not a shallower one. Catch Accel is the one that reads negative.

🔵 Negative Catch Slope

The steepest rate at which acceleration falls going into the catch, as the blades enter and the hull checks. Short label: Neg Slope. Measured in m/s³.

Use: a high value means the check arrives abruptly. Paired with a shallow Catch Accel that is a sharp, well-placed entry. Paired with a deep Catch Accel it is the boat being stopped rather than caught.

🔵 Catch Accel

The peak backward acceleration at the catch, in m/s² - how deep the check goes. Always a negative number. This is the figure displayed a third of the way in from the left, at the bottom of the stroke curve.

Use: closer to zero is a lighter check and less speed thrown away at the front end. Watch it against Recovery Loss, as both describe boat speed being lost rather than gained. Read off the plotted curve, so the number always matches the trough you can see. Do not compare it across boat classes.

🔵 Positive Catch Slope

How fast acceleration rises out of the catch as the drive load comes on. Higher means a sharper, more aggressive catch. Short label: Pos Slope. Measured in m/s³.

Shown as a rolling average with the session peak alongside.

Use: rising slope as rate increases means you're adapting well. Falling slope means the catch is being sacrificed for cadence. A big gap between average and peak means you can produce a good catch but not repeat it.

🔵 Catch Dur

The width of the check, from the point deceleration reaches 30% of its peak depth until acceleration crosses back through zero. Not the whole drive. Accelerometer-derived and essentially unaffected by boat speed, which makes it unusually readable across a session - rate barely moves it.

Use: shorter can mean a sharper build to peak force, a quicker, more connected catch and not rowing the oar in. It is not a measure of length or full drive time.

🔵 Catch Consistency

Standard deviation of recent Positive Catch Slope values. Lower is more repeatable.

Use: consistency degrading late in a session is an early fatigue signal - it usually shows before pace drops.

🔵 Drive Time

Time from peak drive acceleration to blade exit - the back half of the drive, complementing Catch Dur. Same accelerometer source, so it covers every stroke regardless of GPS.

Use: add it to Catch Dur for a full-drive estimate. Watching the split between the two shows whether you're changing the front or the back of the drive.

🔵 Impulse

Total positive acceleration through the drive, integrated as a change in velocity (Δv). Higher means more drive power delivered to the hull. It's a total, not a peak.

Use: condition-independent measure of how hard the boat got pushed. Impulse rising at constant pace means more effort for the same result. Impulse falling at improving pace means you're getting more efficient.

🔵 Drive Accel

Peak forward acceleration during the drive, catch to finish, in m/s².

Use: the sharp end of the drive. Read alongside Impulse - high peak with modest impulse means a spike rather than a sustained push. This is the figure displayed on the top left of the stroke curve.

🔵 Recovery Accel

Peak forward acceleration during the recovery, measured after the small drag-dip that follows the finish hands away, in m/s².

Use: how much the boat is being pulled toward the rower in the recovery. At high ratings, this figure should be increasing. This is the figure displayed on the top right of the stroke curve.

🔵 Vel Efficiency (%)

Hydrodynamic efficiency of the boat's speed profile within a single stroke. Drag power rises with the cube of speed, so a boat that surges and slows wastes energy against one holding a constant speed.

  • 100% - a perfectly steady hull
  • 93–97% - typical sculling

Higher is better.

Use: the cleanest single number for run quality. It replaces the old Check Delta, which measured the same underlying thing but as a raw speed swing that meant nothing without context.

19. Limitations and warnings

GPS metrics measure over the ground, not through the water

Pace and DPS measure motion relative to the ground. On a tidal river a 0.3 m/s following current improves your displayed pace by roughly 10 seconds per 500m at a 2:00 baseline. DPS is inflated downstream and deflated upstream for exactly the same reason.

The accelerometer metrics - rate, ratio, Stroke Char, Positive and Negative Catch Slope, Catch Accel, Catch Dur, Drive Time, Impulse, Drive Accel, Recovery Accel, Recovery Loss - are unaffected by current. These are your reliable technique metrics on tidal water.

The mount matters more than anything else

Every accelerometer metric assumes the phone is rigidly attached to the hull. Any independent movement corrupts all of them. A mount that rocks near stroke frequency is particularly bad - it corrupts stroke ratio and Stroke Char specifically, and it does so plausibly enough that you might believe the numbers.

Recalibrate whenever you move the phone.

Axis lock after a long rest

Axis lock can drift after a long stop. The AX pill goes orange when unlocked. If it stays orange after several strokes, row steadily for 20–30 strokes to let the detector relock.

Crew boats

In doubles, quads and eights the accelerometer sees all the rowers superimposed. Stroke detection follows the dominant signal, usually stroke seat. At high rates, adjacent catches can trigger false detections. Catch Slope, Catch Accel and Impulse values are not comparable with single-scull values.

No absolute force measurement

These metrics measure the hull's acceleration response to blade force, not the force itself. The same force produces very different accelerations in a single and an eight. Never compare Catch Slope, Catch Accel, Impulse, Drive Accel or Recovery Loss across boat classes.

Cold and battery

Below about 5°C the phone may throttle its CPU or drop the sensor polling rate. Keep it warm before launching. Start long sessions at full charge, and see the battery notes if you're connected to a squad.

Squad features need mobile data

Not Wi-Fi. Squad, Coach and Viewer all need an active mobile connection on the water. Recording is entirely local and continues regardless - the live view is best-effort. Rower tiles grey out under bridges and in poor signal; that's normal.

Protecting your data

  • Confirm the synced badge before you rely on Drive as your only copy
  • Export JSON immediately after a significant session
  • Tap Finish rather than force-closing the app

20. Troubleshooting

Stroke detection not triggering Recalibrate with the phone in the mount and the boat still. If the AX pill stays orange for more than a minute the signal is too weak on every axis - check the mount is rigid and flat.

AX pill shows the wrong axis Settings → SYSTEM SETTINGS → SURGE AXIS → FORCE X or FORCE Y. Only do this if AUTO is genuinely reading it wrong; the automatic pin is correct almost always. The best way to check is watching the stroke curve. If it is significantly wrong, it's probably an axis detection issue.

Pace high, or dropping out GPS accuracy must be better than 25m. Under bridges and near tall buildings it will drop out briefly. On tidal water pace is over-ground speed - that's expected, not a fault.

Recording stopped at 20 minutes Basic tier limit. Upgrade to Pro for unlimited recording.

Tiles say PRO - Upgrade to unlock That metric is Pro-only. The tile stays visible so you can see what's there.

Drive sync failing Check Drive is connected (). Pending sessions retry automatically when the connection comes back. Export JSON as a backup if it keeps failing.

Session missing from the phone HOME → LOG → Sync with Drive.

Phone says storage is full HOME → SETTINGS → YOUR DATA → STORAGE…. It reports what BoatRun is using and offers to free space by dropping the on-phone copy of sessions older than 30 days that are already backed up on Drive. Nothing is deleted - they stay in your log and download again when opened.

A session opens slowly, or says it needs signal Its full copy has been freed off the phone and is being fetched from Drive. Anything from the last 30 days is always held whole on the phone, so this only affects older rows.

Deleted a session and want it back You can't. Deleting removes it from Drive and every device at once, which is why both apps ask you to confirm. Export sessions you might want later - raw data export is free forever.

Session still showing in the Analyser after deleting it on the phone Reload the Analyser. The Drive file is deleted straight away, so a refresh clears it - unless you deleted while offline, in which case it goes on your next sync.

HR strap won't connect Wet the contacts, put it on before connecting, and make sure no watch or other app has already claimed it.

No rowers appearing in Coach Every phone needs mobile data, not Wi-Fi. Confirm the squad code matches exactly. Check the connection status in Settings.

Rower tiles going grey No recent data. Check that rower is actually recording and not paused. Intermittent grey under bridges is normal.

Battery draining fast You're probably connected to a squad. See the battery notes.

Android: recording stops when the screen is off or the app is in the background Settings → Apps → BoatRun → Battery → Unrestricted. Samsung phones also need BoatRun removed from Sleeping apps in Device care → Battery → Background usage limits.

Android: HR strap won't pair Android needs Nearby devices permission as well as Bluetooth. Settings → Apps → BoatRun → Permissions → Nearby devices → Allow. Location must also be on — Android ties Bluetooth scanning to it.

Analyser chart looks empty All series may be toggled off - click any summary card to bring one back. Or you're zoomed into a gap; double-click the chart to reset.

No Personal Bests showing Run Scan All for Records on the Analyser home screen.

Strava upload not appearing Pro only, and Strava must be linked in Settings. For a manual upload, export FIT and add it to Strava as an activity of type Rowing.
