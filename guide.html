1. Getting started and where things are

New to BoatRun? Start with the Quick Start. It walks you through installing the app, signing in and recording your first session, for whichever role you are - Row, Cox, Erg or Coach. About five minutes, no settings to change.

This guide is the full reference. The first five sections set you up; after that each section covers one feature. If something isn't working, go straight to Troubleshooting.

Your first session, in order

  1. Install the app and pick your role on the launch screen - Roles.
  2. Sign in and link Google Drive - Signing in, Drive and sync.
  3. Mount the phone flat, facing the stern - Mounting the phone.
  4. Connect anything you need with the chips at the top of the LIVE screen, then press Start - The live screen and Recording a session.
  5. Finish, name the session, and it backs itself up - Session log.

2. Signing in, Drive and sync CHANGED

Signing in is two short steps, and the app walks you through both. Tap Sign in at the top right of HOME, or the SIGN IN · STEP 1 OF 2 button on the launch screen.

  1. Step 1 - your BoatRun account. Continue with Apple or Continue with Google. This is who you are. It carries your plan between your phone, a new phone and the Analyser, and anchors your free trial so reinstalling doesn't restart it.
  2. Step 2 - link Google Drive. Offered straight after step 1; the button reads LINK DRIVE · STEP 2 OF 2 until it's done. This is where your files go - a private, hidden folder in your own Google Drive.

When both are done the button reads Drive linked. Tap it any time to open Account & Plan: your account, the linked Apple / Google sign-ins, Sign out, and a Google Drive section with Sync now and Unlink Drive.

Why both

  • Without the account a subscription bought on your phone stays on that phone, and the Analyser won't recognise it.
  • Without Drive nothing syncs. Sessions live only on the phone, there is no backup, nothing comes back on a new phone, and templates, workouts, sectors and lines can't move between the app and the Analyser.

Apple and Google

Signing in with Google and with Apple separately creates two different accounts, because your Apple ID and Google account almost certainly use different email addresses. Use the same one on every device. Once signed in, Account & Plan offers Link Apple account or Link Google account - one tap, and it's a spare key to the same account. Apple sign-in isn't offered on Android.

When it syncs

  • Changes you make go up straight away. If you're offline they wait and go when you have signal.
  • The app pulls changes at launch, when you come back to it, and when you open the Log - no more than every 5 minutes, and never while you're recording.
  • The Analyser pulls when it opens. ↻ Sync with app on its start page fetches straight away.
  • Every finished session uploads by itself. On a new phone, sign in and link Drive and your sessions come back on their own. You never need to sync by hand. Sync now (Drive linked → Account & Plan) is only there for when something you expect hasn't arrived.

Strava

3. Basic, Pro and Coach

Free trial

Every new install gets 30 days of everything - Pro and Coach, granted by the app. No card, nothing to cancel. The launch screen counts down the days. Sign in and the start date is kept with your account, so reinstalling doesn't restart it.

When it expires, you drop to Basic. Nothing is deleted and nothing is locked away - all your recorded sessions, exports and raw data stay fully accessible.

What Basic gives you

  • Pace, Stroke Rate, DPS and Heart Rate tiles, plus every time, distance and average tile
  • 20 minute recording limit per session - a warning with 2 minutes left, then the session finishes and saves itself
  • Full session log and calendar, full Drive backup
  • Every export - JSON, CSV, TCX and FIT - always free
  • Join a squad with a code
  • Analyser single-session analysis, and the Viewer, always free

Raw data is never held hostage. If you recorded it, you can export it.

What Pro adds

Unlimited recording, every stroke metric, the live stroke curve on the water and the erg, stroke templates, custom workouts and planning them on your calendar, sectors and courses with live PB racing, lanes, dividers and hazard warnings including club lines, the Navigation tile, squad erg racing, and automatic Strava sync.

On the same account it also unlocks the Analyser: multi-session comparison, the video overlay, opening a whole squad at once, and building stroke templates.

What Coach adds

Coach includes Pro, so you don't buy both. It adds squad codes with QR and password, the live squad map and rower tiles, the SQUAD race view, rower detail with live curve, REQUEST CURVE, LAP ALL BOATS, pushing workouts and templates to the squad, the squad training calendar, squad erg races with a synchronised start, in-app video tagged to a rower, and collating every rower's session. Rowers join free with the code - one Coach subscription covers the squad. See Coach mode.

5. Mounting the phone CHANGED

Orientation

Portrait or landscape both work. What matters is that the phone is flat, screen up, facing the stern (toward the rower), and rigidly attached to the hull. Newer phones (iPhone 15 and newer, and most recent flagship Android handsets) have dual-band GPS, which tracks noticeably better. Signal reflected off water, carbon fibre and metal fittings degrades GPS; a rubber foam mat under the mount helps, particularly on older phones.

  • Portrait - the app uses the Y axis for surge
  • Landscape - the app uses the X axis for surge

The surge axis is set to AUTO: it follows the screen, and switches if you rotate the phone. You don't have to tell it anything. Only if the AX pill reads wrong, force it in SETTINGS → SYSTEM SETTINGS → SURGE AXIS (LANDSCAPE or PORTRAIT).

In landscape, if the phone is turned the wrong way round the app shows ⚠ FLIP PHONE 180° across the top. Turn it round.

Calibration - automatic

There is no calibration step. From the moment the app opens it keeps a record of the phone's movement. When you press Start it picks the quietest 8 seconds of the last minute and uses them to set the stroke-detection threshold, the mount angle and the rowing axis. It fine-tunes again when you start rowing, and at every stop (no strokes for 15 seconds and nearly stationary).

  • For the best start: mount the phone, sit still for a few seconds, then press Start.
  • If the boat was never still (the crew was already paddling), it uses the last good calibration from this phone, or sensible defaults, and corrects itself at your first stop.
  • Moved the phone, changed its angle or switched boats? Nothing to do - just sit still for a few seconds before Start.

Tilt

Flat is best. The app compensates for mount angle, but less reliably the further you go.

  • 0–10° - no meaningful loss of accuracy
  • 10–30° - works, accuracy degrades gradually
  • Over 30° - not supported; the surge signal gets mixed with heave and metrics become unreliable

Cox mode

In Cox role the phone faces the bow instead of the stern. If you forget to select Cox, the giveaway is an unusual stroke curve like this one.

Position in the boat

Closest to the rigger gives the strongest stroke signal. Anywhere rigid is workable. What ruins it is a mount that flexes or rocks - any movement between phone and hull corrupts every accelerometer metric, and a mount that wobbles near stroke frequency corrupts stroke ratio and Stroke Char in particular.

7. Recording a session

Starting - when the clock actually begins

Pressing START puts the app in a ready state rather than starting the clock, so time spent tied up or backing down doesn't land in your session. Three things release it, whichever comes first:

  • You move about 20 metres from where you pressed START.
  • You pick up speed - two GPS readings above walking pace.
  • You take two strokes. The quickest, and the only one that backdates the clock a couple of seconds so the first stroke belongs to the session.

Laps

Tap LAP to mark a split. Each lap records time, distance, pace, rate and HR.

Blue - LAPNormal split, or advance to the next workout step. Takes effect the instant you press it.
Light blue - LAP READYPressing will split and arm ready mode. Offered whenever the boat is sitting still, or when the next workout step waits for rowing.
Light green - READYReady mode is armed. The lap clock is held until you start rowing.

Ready mode

Press LAP READY while sitting at the start. The previous lap closes; the new one does not begin. It starts on its own after two strokes at a real rate, backdated about 2 seconds. A single bump won't trigger it. Press the green READY button to start the lap immediately instead.

The lap summary

After each lap a card shows the lap's time, distance, pace and rate. A green bar counts down until it closes.

  • Tap the card to close it early.
  • Drag the round handle on the bar to change how long it stays up - the full bar is one minute, and where you drop it becomes the new timeout.
  • Or set it in DISPLAY → Screen Settings → LAP SUMMARY TIMEOUT, 0–60 seconds (default 6). 0 turns the summary off.

The sector result card works the same way - see Sectors and courses.

Protecting the screen from splashes

Use the LOCK button to stop water changing screens. You'll need to unlock to press LAP, change tabs or finish. For more protection, use Guided Access on iPhone (triple-click the side button) or Screen Pinning on Android.

Stopping and finishing

STOP pauses, RESUME continues, FINISH ends and saves. Name the session, or tap NO NAME. The app autosaves regularly; if it closes mid-session (battery, crash, anything) the recording is recovered next time you open it.

9. Stroke templates (Pro) CHANGED

A stroke template is a target stroke shape built from your own best strokes. It is drawn as a dashed line behind your live curve, continuously re-timed to whatever rate you're rowing, so you can see straight away where your stroke differs from the one you want.

The workflow: build it in the Analyser → tap Save → it arrives on your phone by itself → arm it → tap its pill on the curve. No files to move.

1. Build it in the Analyser

You need a session with steady pieces at the rates you want - for example a rate ladder at 18, 22, 26, 30 and 34. The Analyser and the phone must be signed in to the same account with Drive linked.

  1. Open the session. In the Curve Comparison pane, open the STROKE TEMPLATE drop-down and choose ＋ New template…. The Create / Edit Stroke Template panel opens. Type a name.
  2. Add a rate. Tap the yellow ＋ Select strokes on the chart. The panel closes and a yellow bar across the top shows the template's name and the rates it has so far. On the Stroke Chart, drag across at least 3 steady strokes at one rate, then tap ＋ Add to template · 24.0 spm (it shows the rate of what you selected).
  3. Repeat for each rate you want - for example 20, 24, 28 and 32. Adding a rate within a quarter of a stroke of one already in the template replaces it. Tap Done on the yellow bar to go back to the panel.
  4. Check the rates. The panel lists each rate with its drive % and stroke count. × removes one. Already pinned some curve groups? Or add a curve group you already pinned lets you add those instead.
  5. Preview. Drag the PREVIEW AT … SPM slider. Grey is the curves you recorded, purple is what the phone will draw at that rate, green dashed is the finish. Above your highest rate the drive is held at its recorded length and only the recovery shortens.
  6. Save. Tap Save (it changes to Saved ✓). The template goes to your Drive and appears on your phone the next time the app syncs - straight away when you next open the app.
  • On-water and erg never mix. A template takes the kind of the first strokes you add - accelerometer curves from a boat, or PM5 force curves from an erg.
  • The STROKE TEMPLATE drop-down lists every template open here and every one already on your phone (through Drive), plus Open template file… and ＋ New template…. Pick one to make it the active template. Edit reopens the panel; Close puts it away. "· unsaved" means changes not yet saved.
  • Check it against a curve: pin a curve group (drag a selection, ＋ Add to curves), then tap its pill to show that curve on its own with the active template drawn over it at the same rate. Tap the pill again, or Show all curves, to go back.
  • With several templates open, the panel's EDITING row shows them; the yellow one is the one you're editing.
  • Export file saves a template file to send to someone. Delete removes the template from the phone too.
  • Not signed in to Drive? Save downloads a template file instead, which you can import on the phone.
  • Open template file… opens a file someone has sent you. If you're signed in to Drive it is sent to your phone as well.
  • Creating templates needs Pro. Coach accounts can open and view them.

Overheating

On iPhone, if the phone reports it is overheating in the sun, a ⚠ PHONE OVERHEATING banner counts down 15 seconds before switching to the dark screen, which makes far less heat than white at full brightness.   • A squad link uses the most battery of anything. Leave it disconnected unless your coach needs you live - you can still Send to squad from the log afterwards.

Getting sectors

The SECTORS list

  • Show live sectors - switches the ARM / SKIP pop-up and live sector times on or off.

  • PRE-ARM - see below.

Rowing a sector

  1. Row towards the start, heading the right way. As you approach, a pop-up asks ARM or SKIP.
  2. Once armed, the Workout / Sector tile becomes the sector tile: metres to the start on top, your PB in the middle, the sector name at the bottom.

16. Erg mode

BoatRun talks to a Concept2 PM5 over Bluetooth. The phone is a second screen and a recorder - it is not measuring anything itself. No GPS, no accelerometer, no calibration. Every number comes off the monitor.

Governing principle: wherever the PM5 supplies a value, BoatRun displays it as-is. If your erg screen and phone screen disagree, that is a bug worth reporting.

Connecting

CONNECT ERG on the erg live screen, or HOME → SETTINGS → ERGOMETER (CONCEPT2 PM5) → CONNECT TO ERG.

  1. Wake the PM5 first - press any key or take a stroke. A sleeping monitor won't be found.
  2. Tap CONNECT TO ERG and pick your PM5.
  3. Once connected the PM5 pill turns green and the drag factor (DF) shows in the header.

Heart rate - pair the strap to the erg, not the phone

25. Metrics reference

  • 🟢 Independent - compare across any session, conditions or boat. Accelerometer timing.
  • 🔵 Boat dependent - compare within the same boat.
  • 🔴 Condition dependent - GPS-derived. Tide, current and wind move the number.

🔴 Pace

Boat speed in your chosen unit. Suppressed when GPS accuracy is worse than 25 m or the boat is barely moving. Don't compare across days on tidal water unless conditions and direction match.

🔴 Moving Pace

Pace averaged only over the periods the boat was moving. The better number for interval training.

🔴 Distance Per Stroke (DPS)

Boat travel per stroke. Typically 7–11 m in a single. At equal pace, higher DPS means a lower rating; a mid-session drop at steady rate usually means fatigue.

🟢 Stroke Rate

Strokes per minute, from recent catch-to-catch intervals. Clears after a rest and returns after two new catches.

🟢 Stroke Ratio

Recovery time ÷ drive time. About 2.0–2.5 at training rates, 1.5–1.8 at race rates. If it collapses as rate climbs, you're shortening the recovery instead of quickening the drive.

🟢 Stroke Char (%)

Where peak drive acceleration falls within the drive: 0% = boat starts accelerating after the catch, 100% = blade exit. Under 40% front-loaded, 40–60% mid-drive, over 60% back-loaded. Smoothed over 5 strokes. On the erg it is where peak force falls within the stroke.

🟢 Recovery Loss

Speed lost to deceleration in the recovery, per second of recovery. Lower is better.

🔵 The catch group

  1. Negative Catch Slope - the gradient down into the check
  2. Catch Accel - the depth of the trough (reads negative)
  3. Positive Catch Slope - the gradient back out
  4. Catch Dur - the width of the trough, from 30% of its depth to back through zero

A steep Negative Slope with a shallow Catch Accel is a sharp, well-placed entry; with a deep Catch Accel it is the boat being stopped. Rising Positive Slope as rate climbs means you're adapting well. Shorter Catch Dur usually means a quicker, more connected catch.

🔵 Catch Consistency

Variation in recent Positive Catch Slope. Lower is more repeatable; it usually degrades before pace drops.

🔵 Drive Time

Peak drive acceleration to blade exit - the back half of the drive.

🔵 Impulse

Total positive acceleration through the drive (Δv). Rising at constant pace means more effort for the same result.

🔵 Drive Accel

Peak forward acceleration in the drive, m/s². Top left of the curve.

🔵 Recovery Accel

Peak forward acceleration in the recovery, after the small dip following the finish. Top right of the curve. Should rise at high ratings.

🔵 Vel Efficiency (%)

How steady the hull's speed is through the stroke. 100% = perfectly steady; 93–97% typical sculling. Higher is better.

🟢 Heart Rate

From a connected strap, with zone colouring.

Navigation

Not a metric - the lane, divider and hazard readout as a tile. See Lanes on the water.

26. Limitations and warnings

Lanes and hazards are an aid, not a lookout

Warnings depend on GPS, which is typically 3–5 m out and can be much worse under bridges, near buildings and trees, or on older phones. A hazard is only as accurate as where it was drawn, and a warning needs a few seconds of movement to know your heading. Set a larger hazard warning distance where it matters. Keep looking around. BoatRun doesn't know where other boats are and will never keep you clear of them.

GPS metrics measure over the ground

Pace and DPS measure motion relative to the ground. On a tidal river a 0.3 m/s current changes displayed pace by about 10 seconds per 500m at a 2:00 baseline. The accelerometer metrics are unaffected - they are your reliable technique metrics on tidal water.

The mount matters more than anything else

Every accelerometer metric assumes the phone is rigidly attached to the hull. A mount that rocks near stroke frequency corrupts stroke ratio and Stroke Char plausibly enough that you might believe the numbers. After moving the phone, sit still for a few seconds before you press Start so the automatic calibration gets a clean reading.

Axis lock after a long rest

If the AX pill stays orange after several strokes, row steadily for 20–30 strokes to let the detector relock.

Crew boats

In doubles, quads and eights the accelerometer sees all the rowers together; stroke detection follows the dominant signal. Catch Slope, Catch Accel and Impulse are not comparable with single-scull values, and stroke templates must be built per boat class.

No absolute force measurement

These metrics measure the hull's response to blade force, not the force itself. Never compare Catch Slope, Catch Accel, Impulse, Drive Accel or Recovery Loss across boat classes.

Cold, heat and battery

Below about 5°C the phone may throttle. In hot sun it can overheat - see Power and battery. Start long sessions at full charge.

Signal

Squad, Coach and the Viewer need an active mobile connection on the water, as do club line updates and the course list. Recording, and warnings for lines already on the phone, are entirely local and carry on without signal. Sync waits for signal.
